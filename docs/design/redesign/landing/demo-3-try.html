Under the hood

Why it reacts so fast.

Every reading is split in two the moment it arrives. One copy goes straight to the decision. The other is saved for history, in bulk, without slowing the decision down.

Decision · milliseconds

Rules in memoryno database on the way
Commandsconfirmed, with an expiry

History · in bulk

BufferRedis stream
Time-series databaseTimescaleDB, rollups, retention

In plain words: deciding never waits for saving. A busy moment for the database can't delay a fan, a pump or an alarm.

For your IT team: one Linux server with Docker Compose runs 500–1,000 devices. Postgres row-level security keeps workspaces apart, credentials are hashed with argon2id, and the restore procedure is tested.

FAQ

Before you ask.

Can I turn the safety settings off?
No. You choose the numbers (a hold time of 0 seconds is allowed for sensors that don't bounce), but they're always part of the rule, and the editor warns you when a rule that switches hardware has none.
What if a sensor stops sending?
Its readings are marked stale and the rule treats them as unknown: it neither fires nor switches anything back off. You can get an alert when a device goes offline.
And if the site's internet drops?
Offline devices can't take commands. When they reconnect they get the latest intended state, and commands that are too old expire instead of running late.
Does it work with equipment I already have?
Yes. Anything that speaks MQTT connects directly; Modbus, OPC UA and vendor clouds come in through a small edge connector on your network.
Where is my data stored?
In IO Driven's cloud, in a dedicated instance, or on your own server. The device side is identical in all three.
