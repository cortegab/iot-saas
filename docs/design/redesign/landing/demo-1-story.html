How it works

Four steps, repeated every time a sensor speaks.

You describe what should happen in plain sentences. IO Driven does the watching, day and night, and tells you what it did. Each step has a short technical note for whoever runs the system.

1

Sense

Your devices send readings like temperature, tank level or door open/closed, as often as you choose.

Technical note

MQTT over TLS to an EMQX broker. Each device has its own credential, stored hashed, and can only publish to its own topics. HTTP is available as a fallback.

2

Decide

Every reading is checked against your rules right away, across one device or many: "if the tank is low and the pump is on…"

Technical note

Rules are held in memory and evaluated before anything is written to the database. No polling and no scheduled jobs sit between a reading and the decision.

3

Act

A rule can switch an output (fan, pump, valve, light), email the right people, notify phones or call another system.

Technical note

Commands go out with QoS 1 and an expiry time, and the intended state is kept by the broker, so a device that was offline catches up when it reconnects.

4

Confirm and record

The device confirms it did it. Everything lands in history and on your dashboards, and anything that failed is listed with the reason.

Technical note

History goes to TimescaleDB in batches on a separate path, with 1-minute and 1-hour rollups for charts and retention enforced in the database.

Safety

Built for equipment that can break.

Sensors are noisy. A simple "above 30 °C, turn on" rule would switch a relay on and off dozens of times an hour and wear it out. Every rule in IO Driven has three brakes built in, always visible and never optional.

Hold time

The condition must stay true for a while (say 10 seconds) before anything happens. A brief spike is ignored.

Hysteresis

A safety margin before turning back. It turns on at 30 °C, but only resets once it's back below 28 °C.

Minimum interval

A floor on how often a rule can act, for example once a minute at most, whatever the data does.

Without the brakes: the line above crosses 30 °C six times. That's six on/off cycles in a few minutes. With them: the fan turns on once, after the reading stays high, and turns off once, after it's clearly cooled down. If a sensor goes quiet, nothing switches. Missing data is treated as "unknown", never as "all clear".

Use cases

What people connect to it.

Anything that can report a number or a yes/no, and anything that can be switched. A few typical setups:

Agriculture

Greenhouses

Temperature, humidity and soil moisture. Fans, vents and irrigation valves.

"Too hot for 10 s → fan on, until it's back below 28 °C."

Water

Tanks and pumps

Level, pressure and flow. Pumps that must never run dry.

"Tank below 20 % → stop the pump and keep it stopped until someone checks."

Food

Cold rooms

Fridge and freezer temperatures, door contacts and compressor state.

"Door open for 5 min → alert the shift on their phones."

Industry

Machines and lines

Vibration, current and run hours from existing PLCs through an edge connector.

"Vibration high on any motor → email maintenance."

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Do I need to know how to program?
No. For supported ESP32 boards you flash ready-made firmware from the browser, and rules are built from recipes and plain sentences. Developers can still write their own firmware against the device contract.
What happens if the internet drops at the site?
A device that's offline can't receive commands, and no system can change that. When it reconnects, it immediately gets the latest intended state, and commands that are too old expire instead of running late.
Can I use the sensors and PLCs I already have?
Yes, through an edge connector: a small program on your network that reads Modbus, OPC UA or a vendor's cloud and forwards it. To IO Driven it looks like any other device.
Is there a phone app?
Yes, for Android first. It shows dashboards, controls and alerts, sends push notifications, and sets up a new board's Wi-Fi over Bluetooth.
How long is data kept?
It depends on your agreement. Retention is enforced in the database itself, and long-term charts use minute and hour summaries so history stays fast.
How is one customer's data kept from another's?
Every row is tagged with its workspace and protected by row-level security in Postgres, so even a bug in a query can't show another workspace's data.
